\title{Memory Is the Defence: Physics-Gated Continuum-Memory Receivers for Poisoning-Resilient Test-Time Learning}

\author{Abraheem~Rashid%
\thanks{A. Rashid is with the Department of Computer Science, Brunel University London, London, U.K. (e-mail: 2546393@brunel.ac.uk).}%
\thanks{Code, configuration files, all 1\,926 run records, the statistics and the scripts that regenerate every table, figure and number of this paper are available at \url{https://github.com/abraheemrashid99-sys/Citradel-Rx}; the COST2100 traces are those released with \cite{raviv2023}.}}

\markboth{IEEE Transactions on Wireless Communications}{Rashid: Memory Is the Defence}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Neural receivers that learn at test time adapt to the channel from the pilots of every
coherence block, so the pilot stream is a training set that an adversary can write to: a
perturbation shaped through the receiver's own adaptation step destroys online and
meta-learned DeepSIC receivers, and meta-learning retains the poison. This paper proposes
Citadel-Rx, a DeepSIC receiver whose adaptable parameters are kept at three nested
time-scales: a fast level fitted on every block and two slow levels updated every 8 and
32 blocks through a gated average of the faster states. Three label-free
physics-consistency gates, each a ratio against a model-based reference on the same
block (linear-model residual, CRC pass rate, channel innovation), decide what may be
consolidated; a rejected block is reverted and its data are detected with the slow
memory. For the algorithm as implemented, the displacement that a bounded attack causes
in the slowest level over $W$ blocks is proved to be bounded by the fast level's
displacement times $1-(1-\eta^{(K)})^{\lceil W/C_K\rceil}$, and the bound is verified with
measured constants. In 1\,926 recorded runs over four channel families, $4\times4$ and
$8\times8$ systems, three Doppler rates and five attackers, Citadel-Rx keeps its clean
symbol error rate at every budget from $-10$ to $+10$\,dB signal-to-interference ratio
(attack-window SER $0.026$--$0.034$ against $0.28$--$0.97$ for online DeepSIC and
$0.24$--$0.86$ for Meta-DeepSIC, all paired differences significant after Holm
correction), rejects $86$--$99\%$ of poisoned blocks with at most $1.7\%$ false rejections,
and without attack halves the symbol error rate of online DeepSIC at 10\,dB. The limits
of the defence are established as well: a floor near $10^{-3}$ where the clean error rate
is $10^{-4}$, and no clean gain over LMMSE on a nearly diagonal channel.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{D}{eep} receivers replace or augment the model-based blocks of a MIMO
detector with neural networks trained on data \cite{oshea2017,honkala2021,samuel2019,
shlezinger2023}. Since a network trained in simulation meets a different channel in the
field, the strongest of them adapt at test time: online DeepSIC \cite{shlezinger2021}
re-trains its per-user networks on the pilots of every coherence block, and Meta-DeepSIC
\cite{raviv2023} meta-learns an initialisation from the pilots of past blocks so that the
per-block fit converges faster. Their adaptation data are the only labelled symbols the
receiver sees: a few hundred pilot bits per user per block.

The security problem addressed here is the following. An adversary within range of the
base station adds a perturbation to the received pilot part of a block, with an energy
budget expressed as a signal-to-interference ratio (SIR) over the pilots. Wu
\emph{et al.} \cite{wu2024} showed that a perturbation shaped through an unrolled copy
of the receiver's adaptation step raises the post-adaptation symbol error rate (SER) of
online DeepSIC by an order of magnitude, that a perturbation crafted on a surrogate
receiver still transfers, and that Meta-DeepSIC, which keeps the poisoned pilots in its
support set, does not recover after the attack stops. They call for receivers that are
secure by construction; none has been reported. Four properties of the problem shape the
design and the evaluation. The receiver cannot verify its adaptation
data, because the pilots are the labels; what it can verify is physical consistency:
whether its decisions fit a linear channel model, whether the CRC of the decoded data
passes, and whether the estimated channel moved as a channel does. The harm of poisoning
lies not in the attacked block alone but in what the receiver keeps, so a defence must
protect the memory and not only the decision. An attacker who knows the defence will try
to pass its tests, so the tests must be tied to the attacker's own objective. And the
value of a defence can only be established against the attacks of the literature, under
the same channel realisations as the baselines, over many seeds, with the clean cost of
the defence reported alongside its benefit.

Prior work addresses these properties separately (Table~\ref{tab:pos}). Test-time
adaptation in vision has its own poisoning problem \cite{wu2023tta}, and its defences
replace batch statistics or gradients by robust estimates \cite{medbn}; they assume
i.i.d. batches and, as shown below, do not transfer to a receiver that sees one correlated
block at a time. Byzantine-robust aggregation \cite{blanchard2017} needs many gradient
vectors per step, which a receiver with one pilot block does not have. Certified defences
against data poisoning \cite{steinhardt2017} bound the loss of a convex learner under a
fraction of corrupted points; here the setting is sequential, the learner is a network,
and the corruption is a bounded perturbation of every point in a block.
Corruption-robust online learning \cite{lykouris2018} supplies the additive accounting
of Section~\ref{sec:theory}. Nested learning \cite{behrouz2025nested} and Titans
\cite{behrouz2025titans} keep memory at several update frequencies but do not decide
what enters the slow memory, and a single test-time memory is, as the ablation shows, as
vulnerable as online DeepSIC. No published receiver keeps memory at several rates, gates
what enters the slow rates on physics the attacker cannot forge without giving up its
objective, and carries a retention guarantee checked against measurements.

The contributions of this paper are as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A three-timescale receiver} (Section~\ref{sec:citadel}): a fast level with
a delta-rule learner and a surprise-generated step size, slow levels at periods aligned
with the coherence structure of the channel, gated consolidation, a proximal anchor that
pulls the fast level toward the slow projection, and a gated fallback that detects a
rejected block with the uncorrupted memory.
\item \textbf{Relative physics gates} (Section~\ref{sec:gates}): three label-free tests
whose statistics are ratios against a model-based reference computed on the same block,
so that a channel that merely degrades does not look like a poisoned adaptation. The CRC
gate's threshold is derived, not tuned, from a target excess SER
(Proposition~\ref{prop:crc}), and a relative slack keeps it meaningful at $10^{-3}$
operating points.
\item \textbf{A retention certificate for the implemented algorithm}
(Section~\ref{sec:theory}): the exposure of the slowest level to a bounded attack is
divided by its period and multiplied by the miss rate of the gate
(Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}), with a regret decomposition in which the adversary's term is
additive (Theorem~\ref{thm:regret}). Every constant is measured and the bound is drawn
against paired adversarial/clean memory trajectories.
\item \textbf{A reproducible, paired evaluation} (Sections~\ref{sec:setup}--\ref{sec:results}):
seven receivers, five attackers, four channel families, two system sizes, three Doppler
rates and three to five seeds; bootstrap intervals and Holm-corrected Wilcoxon tests on
per-block differences paired on the channel realisation; every number regenerated from
1\,926 recorded runs by one script each.
\end{enumerate}
The evaluation also establishes where the defence does \emph{not} help: at clean SER near
$10^{-4}$ the attacked window sits near $10^{-3}$, because the few poisoned blocks that
pass the gate dominate; on a nearly diagonal channel the clean gain over LMMSE vanishes;
and the first calibration of the relative CRC slack raised false rejections to
$15$--$20\%$ before it was corrected.

Section~\ref{sec:model} presents the system and threat model, Section~\ref{sec:citadel}
the receiver, Section~\ref{sec:theory} the guarantees, Section~\ref{sec:setup} the
experimental setup, Sections~\ref{sec:results} and \ref{sec:discussion} the results and
their scope, and Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.

\section{System Model and Threat Model}\label{sec:model}
This section fixes the uplink, the receiver under attack, the attacker and the metrics by
which every method is judged.

\subsection{Uplink and Adaptive Receiver}
$U$ single-antenna users transmit QPSK symbols to a base station with $N$ antennas. Time
is divided into coherence blocks $b=1,2,\dots$; within block $b$ the channel $\bm
H_b\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times U}$ is constant and the received vector at symbol slot $n$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:channel}
\bm y_n = f\!\left(\bm H_b \bm s_n\right)+\bm w_n,\qquad \bm w_n\sim\mathcal{CN}(\bm 0,\sigma^2\bm I),
\end{equation}
with $f$ the identity (linear channel) or the memoryless power-amplifier nonlinearity
$f(x)=\tanh(0.5x)$ applied per antenna, as in \cite{raviv2023}. Each block carries $N_p$
pilot symbols per user, known to the receiver, followed by $N_d$ data symbols framed in
CRC-protected sub-blocks of $L$ symbols (Table~\ref{tab:setup}). Four channel families
are used (Section~\ref{sec:setup}).

The receiver is DeepSIC \cite{shlezinger2021}: $Q$ soft-interference-cancellation
iterations, each a per-user multilayer perceptron that maps the received vector and the
other users' current soft decisions to a posterior over the user's symbol. Its
parameters $\theta$ are adapted on the pilots of every block,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:adapt}
\theta_b=\Phi(\theta_{b-1};\bm Y_b),
\end{equation}
by $T$ steps of a first-order optimiser on the pilot cross-entropy, and then detect the
data of the block. Online DeepSIC starts every block from $\theta_{b-1}$; Meta-DeepSIC
starts from an initialisation meta-learned by MAML \cite{finn2017} on a buffer of past
pilot blocks, refreshed every five blocks \cite{raviv2023}.

\subsection{Threat Model}
The adversary adds $\bm E_b$ to the received pilot matrix $\bm Y_b\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times
N_p}$ of block $b$, with $\norm{\bm E_b}_F\le\epsilon_b$ set by the SIR over the pilot
block,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:budget}
\epsilon_b=\norm{\bm Y_b}_F\,10^{-\mathrm{SIR}/20},
\end{equation}
so that SIR $0$\,dB spends as much energy as all $U$ users together; the data part is not
touched. The attacker knows the pilots, which are standardised, and observes the channel
through its own receiver. Following and extending \cite{wu2024}, four attackers are
considered.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{A1, availability.} Projected gradient ascent \cite{madry2018} on the
post-adaptation data loss, differentiated through an unrolled copy of the victim's
adaptation map \eqref{eq:adapt} (white-box), or crafted on a surrogate online DeepSIC
and injected (transfer). A receiver without an adaptation map (LMMSE) is attacked through
the surrogate.
\item \emph{A2, targeted drift.} A1 with an objective that raises one user's loss while
keeping the others' low, on a Jakes channel, so that the poison resembles a plausible
channel change.
\item \emph{A3, boiling frog.} Many blocks of small perturbations ($5\%$ of the pilot
energy) that push the receiver toward a spoofed channel basin while keeping the SER
inside any tolerance a detector might use.
\item \emph{Gate-aware.} A1 with a penalty on the defence's gate statistics in the
attacker's objective: an attacker who knows the defence and tries to pass it.
\end{itemize}
A \emph{data-jamming control} spends the same energy on the data part of the block
instead of the pilots; it separates persistent poisoning from plain interference.

\subsection{Metrics}
The SER is the fraction of symbol slots in which at least one user is wrong, the metric
of \cite{shlezinger2021,raviv2023,wu2024}, reported as a mean over the attack window
(blocks 30--69), before it (pre) and after it (post). The recovery time is the number of
blocks after the attack until the 3-block moving average of the SER returns within $10\%$
of the pre-attack mean (30: not within the run). The gate is characterised by its
rejection rate of poisoned blocks and its false-rejection rate of clean blocks. The
degradation $\Delta_{\rm dB}$ is the extra SNR needed under attack to reach the clean SER
\cite{wu2024}. The paired displacement $D_k(W)=\norm{\theta^{(k)}_{\rm adv}-\theta^{(k)}_{\rm
clean}}$ of memory level $k$ is measured between an attacked and a clean run on the same
channel. Every number of the paper is regenerated from the recorded runs by one script
each; finished records are skipped on re-run, so every stage of the study is resumable.

\subsection{Levels, Periods and the Fast Learner}
The adaptable parameters $\theta\in\mathbb{R}^d$ (all DeepSIC weights, $d=15\,628$ for
$4\times4$) are kept at $K=3$ levels $\theta^{(1)},\theta^{(2)},\theta^{(3)}$ with periods
$C=(1,8,32)$ blocks, each a full copy. The fast level is adapted on every block by $T=300$
steps of
\begin{align}
\theta&\leftarrow\theta-\eta_b\delta\,\theta\,\bm\phi\bm\phi^{\!\top}\quad\text{(delta-rule decay, per weight matrix)},\label{eq:delta}\\
\theta&\leftarrow\theta-\eta_b\lambda(\theta-a_b)\quad\text{(proximal pull to the anchor)},\label{eq:prox}\\
\theta&\leftarrow\mathrm{Adam}\big(\theta,\nabla_\theta\ell_b(\theta)\big),\label{eq:adam}
\end{align}
where $\ell_b$ is the pilot cross-entropy of the DeepSIC stage being adapted (stages are
adapted sequentially as in \cite{shlezinger2021}), $\bm\phi$ is the normalised mean input
of the stage (the direction along which the delta rule forgets), $\delta=0.1$,
$\lambda=0.5$, and $a_b=\sum_{k\ge2}w_k\theta^{(k)}_{b-1}$ with $w=(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$ is
the \emph{anchor}, the slow projection. The step size is generated by the receiver's own
surprise: with $S_b=\norm{\nabla_\theta\ell_b(\theta_{b-1})}^2$ and $\bar S_b$ its
exponential moving average over past blocks, seeded by the median of the first five,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eta}
\eta_b=\eta_{\max}\frac{2r_b}{1+r_b},\qquad r_b=\mathrm{clip}\!\left(S_b/\bar S_b,\tfrac18,8\right),
\end{equation}
so that an unsurprising block receives a small step and a surprising one at most
$2\eta_{\max}$. The rate is bounded, which the analysis requires.

\subsection{Gated Consolidation}
Every $C_{k+1}$ blocks, level $k{+}1$ receives the gated mean of the level-$k$ states
buffered since its last update, each weighted by the gate verdict $\gamma_j\in[0,1]$ of
its block (level 1) or by the mean verdict of its period (level 2):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:consol}
\begin{aligned}
\theta^{(k+1)}&\leftarrow\theta^{(k+1)}+s\Big(\frac{\sum_j\gamma_j\theta^{(k)}_j}{\max(1,\sum_j\gamma_j)}-\theta^{(k+1)}\Big),\\
s&=\eta^{(k+1)}\min\big(1,\textstyle\sum_j\gamma_j\big),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with $\eta^{(2)}=1$ (level 2 is a running mean of the fast level) and $\eta^{(3)}=0.5$.
Eq.~\eqref{eq:consol} is a convex combination (Lemma~\ref{lem:convex}): a level never
leaves the convex hull of what it has been shown. The fast level is anchored continuously
by \eqref{eq:prox} rather than re-set at consolidation instants as in
\cite{behrouz2025nested}; Section~\ref{sec:ablate} shows that this choice is the source
of the clean gain.

\subsection{Rejection, Reversion and Fallback}
If $\gamma_b<\tfrac12$ the block is rejected: the fast update is reverted,
$\theta^{(1)}_b=\theta^{(1)}_{b-1}$; the reverted state enters the buffer with weight
$\gamma_b$; and the data of the block are detected with the anchor, the memory the
attacker has not reached. Reversion protects the memory; the fallback protects the
attacked block itself (Section~\ref{sec:ablate}: without it the attack-window SER is
$0.245$, with it $0.028$). The remaining settings, all read from the configuration file,
are $\eta_{\max}=5\cdot10^{-3}$, a surprise EMA weight of $0.9$ with a five-block warm-up,
gate thresholds $\tau_1=1.25$ and $\tau_3=4$ (the latter floored by the $\chi^2_{0.999}$
quantile per degree of freedom), a soft step of width $0.1\tau$ around every threshold,
and thresholds tightened by up to $30\%$ under surprise.


\subsection{Relative Physics Gates}\label{sec:gates}
Every statistic is a ratio against a reference computed on the same block from the
received data and no labels: the LMMSE detector built on the least-squares pilot channel
estimate $\hat{\bm H}_b$, and the detector obtained with the anchor parameters; the
reference is the better of the two. Ratios are normalised by their exponential moving
average over accepted blocks and compared with a soft threshold, tightened by up to
$30\%$ when the surprise ratio $r_b$ exceeds one. Fig.~\ref{fig:gateflow} gives the
decision inside one block.

\textit{G1, model residual.} With $r(\hat{\bm s})=\tfrac1{N_d}\sum_n\norm{\bm y_n-\hat{\bm
H}_b\hat{\bm s}_n}^2$, the statistic is $r(\hat{\bm s}_{\rm nn})/r(\hat{\bm s}_{\rm ref})$. A
poisoned adaptation produces decisions inconsistent with the linear model. Both terms
contain the same noise and model-mismatch energy, so a PA nonlinearity compresses the
ratio toward one and cannot raise a false alarm (Section~\ref{sec:theory}).

\textit{G2, CRC pass rate.} The fraction $F_{\rm nn}$ of CRC sub-blocks that pass with the
fast level, over the fraction $F_{\rm ref}$ with the reference, against the threshold
$\tau_2$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:crc}. This gate is tied to the availability attacker's
own objective: an attack that raises the SER lowers the pass rate.

\textit{G3, channel prior.} The innovation energy of the LS channel estimate against its
AR(1) prediction, relative to its running level on accepted blocks and floored by the
$\chi^2$ quantile of the degrees of freedom. It targets physically implausible jumps and
is the only gate not fooled by an attacker who keeps the SER low.

\section{Guarantees}\label{sec:theory}
The guarantees are stated for the algorithm as implemented; the full proofs are given in
the theory document of the repository. Let $\Phi(\theta;\bm Y,a)$ denote the block
adaptation map (the fast state after one block, given the pilots and the anchor), and
consider paired runs on the same clean sequence with and without an attack, with
displacements $d_b=\norm{\theta^{(1),\rm adv}_b-\theta^{(1),\rm clean}_b}$ and $D_k(b)$ for
level $k$.

\begin{lemma}[consolidation is a convex combination]\label{lem:convex}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:consol} writes the new level-$(k{+}1)$ state as a convex combination of its
old state and the buffered level-$k$ states, with total weight $s\le\eta^{(k+1)}$ on the
latter. Consequently, if the paired runs have equal verdicts, $D_{k+1}\le\max\{D_{k+1}^{\rm
old},\max_j D_k(j)\}$, and by induction $D_k(b)\le\max_{j\le b}d_j$ for every $k\ge2$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{theorem}[retention certificate]\label{thm:cert}
Let an attack occupy a window of $W$ blocks with equal gate verdicts in the paired runs,
let $n(W)\le\lceil W/C_K\rceil$ be the number of level-$K$ consolidations inside it, and
let $\Delta_f(W)=\max_{\rm window}d_b$ be the largest paired fast-level displacement. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cert}
D_K(W)\le\Delta_f(W)\big(1-(1-\eta^{(K)})^{n(W)}\big)\le\eta^{(K)}\Delta_f(W)n(W).
\end{equation}
If moreover $\norm{\Phi(\theta;\bm Y+\bm E,a)-\Phi(\theta;\bm Y,a)}\le G_\Phi\norm{\bm E}$ and
$\Phi$ is $L_\theta$- and $L_a$-Lipschitz in $\theta$ and $a$ with $L=L_\theta+L_a<1$, then
$\Delta_f(W)\le G_\Phi\epsilon\min\{1,(1-L_\theta)n_W\}/(1-L)$, where $n_W=\rho_{\rm
blk}(1-p^{\rm rej})W$ counts the attacked blocks the gate accepts; if only $L\le1$ holds
(non-expansive map), $\Delta_f(W)\le G_\Phi\epsilon\,n_W$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Level $K$ consolidates $n(W)$ times in the window; each time, by Lemma~\ref{lem:convex},
$D_K\leftarrow(1-s)D_K+s\Delta_f$ with $s\le\eta^{(K)}$, which iterated from $D_K=0$ gives
\eqref{eq:cert}. For the second part, the triangle inequality on $\Phi$ gives $d_b\le
L_\theta d_{b-1}+L_a\max_{k\ge2}D_k(b-1)+G_\Phi\epsilon\chi_b$ with $\chi_b=1$ on attacked
accepted blocks; the lemma and the geometric sum give the bound, and with $L\le1$ the
recursion is non-expansive and adds at most $G_\Phi\epsilon$ per accepted attacked block.
\end{proof}
The first part needs no regularity of the fast learner: the exposure of the slow level
is divided by its period whatever the fast level does. The second part identifies where
the gate enters, through $n_W$, and why rejected blocks inject nothing: they are reverted.
The form $\eta^{(K)}\rho\,\epsilon\,G(1-p^{\rm rej})W/C_K$ holds with $G=G_\Phi/(1-L)$.

\begin{theorem}[corruption-robust dynamic regret]\label{thm:regret}
Let $\ell_b$ be $G_\ell$-Lipschitz in $\theta$ and $R_T(u)=\sum_b\ell_b(\theta_b)-\ell_b(u_b)$
the dynamic regret against a comparator sequence. Under the hypotheses of
Theorem~\ref{thm:cert}, $R^{\rm adv}_T(u)\le R^{\rm clean}_T(u)+G_\ell\,T\,\Delta_f(T)$; without
the anchor ($L_a=0$), $R^{\rm adv}_T(u)\le R^{\rm clean}_T(u)+G_\ell G_\Phi\epsilon\,n_T/(1-L_\theta)$;
and a detector that uses level $k$ pays at most
$G_\ell\Delta_f(T)\min\{T,\eta^{(k)}T^2/(2C_k)\}$.
\end{theorem}
The adversary's contribution is additive to the clean regret, linear in the number of
accepted corrupted blocks and divided by the level period. No rate is claimed for the
clean term of the full-MLP receiver, which is reported empirically; for a
last-layer-adaptable (convex) variant the $O(\sqrt{T(1+P_T)})$ bound of
\cite{zinkevich2003,mokhtari2016} applies with the bounded step \eqref{eq:eta}.

\begin{proposition}[CRC gate]\label{prop:crc}
With i.i.d. symbol errors of probability $p$ in a sub-block of $L$ symbols and an $r$-bit
CRC whose syndrome is uniform on error, $P(\mathrm{pass}\mid p)=(1-p)^L+2^{-r}(1-(1-p)^L)$.
Inverting at $F_{\rm ref}$ gives $p_{\rm ref}$, and the rule ``reject if $F_{\rm nn}/F_{\rm
ref}<\tau_2$'' with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tau2}
\tau_2=\Big(\frac{1-p_{\rm ref}-\delta}{1-p_{\rm ref}}\Big)^{L},\qquad\delta=\max\{\delta_{\min},\rho\,p_{\rm ref}\},
\end{equation}
rejects exactly when the fast level's error probability exceeds the reference's by more
than $\delta$, up to the Hoeffding fluctuation $2e^{-2nt^2}$ of the pass fraction over $n$
sub-blocks \cite{hoeffding1963}.
\end{proposition}
With $n=781$ the gate is deterministic for practical purposes against any attacker that
raises the SER by more than $\delta$, and an attacker that does not has caused less than
$\delta$ of harm. The absolute rule ($\delta\equiv0.01$) is $30\times$ the clean SER at a
$3\cdot10^{-4}$ operating point; the relative rule with $\rho=1$ and
$\delta_{\min}=2\cdot10^{-3}$ (five standard deviations of the pass fraction) follows it.

The residual gate is insensitive to model mismatch for the same reason: writing $\bm
y_n=\hat{\bm H}\bm s_n+\bm\nu_n+\bm m_n$ with $\bm m_n$ the mismatch term, the residual of a
decision with error pattern $\bm e_n$ is $\mathrm{mean}\norm{\hat{\bm
H}\bm e_n}^2+\mathrm{mean}\norm{\bm\nu_n+\bm m_n}^2+O(N_d^{-1/2})$, so the ratio of the two
residuals is monotone in the excess decision-error energy of the fast level and the
mismatch only compresses it toward one.

\section{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:setup}
A fair test needs strong attackers, the receivers of the literature, several channel
families and many seeds, with every receiver seeing the same realisation. The
experiments are: clean SER against SNR ($4$--$16$\,dB) on the four channel families (E2);
A1 white-box and transfer at SIR $-10$ to $10$\,dB with the jamming control on the SED
channel (E3), across SNR (E3snr), on the $\tanh$ PA channel and on COST2100 (E3nl, E3cost);
A2 on Jakes fading with and without G3 (E4); A3 over 300 blocks with $C_K\in\{8,32,128\}$
and a paired clean run (E5); the gate-aware attacker with penalty weights 5, 50 and 500
against gate subsets (E7); the $8\times8$ system (E8); Jakes fading at $f_DT_b=0.01$, $0.05$
and $0.10$ (E9); and sixteen ablation variants. All use 3 seeds; E3 and the 10\,dB clean
cells use 5.


\textit{Baselines.} LMMSE on the least-squares pilot estimate (no adaptation; the
model-based reference); online DeepSIC \cite{shlezinger2021}; Meta-DeepSIC
\cite{raviv2023}; a drift-detection receiver that adapts only when a $z$-test on the
pilot loss fires (the ``adapt less'' strategy); a MedBN-style receiver that replaces the
adaptation gradient by a median of means over four pilot groups \cite{medbn}; and a
single-memory Titans receiver, the same delta-rule fast learner and surprise rate with
$K=1$ and no gates \cite{behrouz2025titans}, which isolates the memory hierarchy from the
fast rule. All DeepSIC-type receivers share the architecture, the pre-training and the
300 adaptation steps per block of Table~\ref{tab:setup}.

\textit{Protocol.} Every receiver is run on the same channel realisation, noise and
attack draw for a given seed (paired design); the attacker re-crafts its perturbation
for each victim. Means over seeds are reported, with 95\% bootstrap confidence intervals
over the (seed, block) pairs (10\,000 resamples \cite{efron1993}) and two-sided Wilcoxon
signed-rank tests \cite{wilcoxon1945} on the paired per-block differences,
Holm-corrected within each table \cite{holm1979}, for the main cells. The attack window, recovery criterion and budget were fixed before the sweeps; the one
change made after seeing results, the relative CRC slack, is reported with its first,
failed calibration (Section~\ref{sec:cost2100}).

\subsection{COST2100 and the Relative Slack}\label{sec:cost2100}
COST2100 at 10\,dB is the one setting in which the first version of the receiver left
its clean trajectory. With the absolute CRC slack ($\delta=0.01$), the attack-window SER
of Citadel-Rx was $0.0022/0.0032/0.0054/0.0122/0.0111$ at SIR $-10$ to $10$\,dB against a
clean $0.0003$, with rejection falling to $0.64$: a one-point slack is $30\times$ the clean
SER, so the attacker can pass G2 while doing damage. The relative slack of
\eqref{eq:tau2} brings the window to $0.0011/0.0012/0.0012/0.0015/0.0028$ (rejection
$0.89/0.90/0.86/0.74/0.40$, no false rejections) and leaves the linear grid unchanged
($0.025$--$0.029$, false rejection $\le2\%$). A first calibration with a floor of $10^{-3}$
had fixed COST2100 but raised linear false rejections to $15$--$20\%$, because the floor
sat inside the statistical resolution of the pass fraction; $2\cdot10^{-3}$ is five
standard deviations of it. On this nearly diagonal channel LMMSE attacked through the
surrogate is itself nearly immune ($0.0010$ at SIR 0\,dB), so Citadel-Rx matches rather
than beats the model-based receiver under attack ($0.0013$; paired difference $+0.0003$,
interval $[-0.0005,+0.0010]$).

\subsection{Cost}
Per block on one CPU worker (eight single-thread workers in parallel, 300 adaptation
steps): LMMSE $0.07$\,s, online DeepSIC $4.0$\,s, drift-detection $9.2$\,s, Meta-DeepSIC
$12.4$\,s, Citadel-Rx $12.2$\,s, single-memory Titans $14.4$\,s, MedBN-style $37.2$\,s.
Citadel-Rx costs the same as Meta-DeepSIC; its overhead is the gate (one LMMSE detection
and one CRC check per block), while the levels cost memory, not compute. The white-box
attacker spends $3.2$\,s of PGD per poisoned block.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
Some conclusions are firmer than others. Against the attacks of \cite{wu2024} and their
stronger variants, the robustness of the receiver is not in doubt: the attacked SER
equals the clean one across budgets, knowledge models, channel families, system sizes
and Doppler rates, with intervals and tests that leave no room for a seed artefact. The ablation identifies which parts matter (the
slow level, the fallback, and G1 or G2) and which do not (period alignment, the delta
decay, the surprise rate, reverting versus keeping a rejected update). This is stated
plainly because it is the finding most likely to be misread: the value of the receiver
lies in its memory and its fallback, not in a cleverer fast learner.

The clean gain is a second contribution, but a conditional one: large where interference
cancellation matters and nil on a nearly diagonal channel where LMMSE is already near
optimal. The ablation attributes it to the proximal anchor, a prior learned across
blocks, which is why the jump re-anchoring of nested learning does not produce it.

The certificate is the structural bound \eqref{eq:cert} with a measured fast-level
displacement; it holds at every window length and exhibits the $1/C_K$ mechanism. The
contraction branch of Theorem~\ref{thm:cert} is not certified for the default receiver,
since the measured $L_\theta+L_a$ exceeds one at every proximal weight; a guarantee with
no measured quantity requires a fast learner that is a joint contraction in its state and
its anchor, which the present design is not.

Two limits are stated as such. At clean SER near $10^{-4}$ the attacked window sits near
$10^{-3}$: the few poisoned blocks that pass the gate set a floor that a different slack
does not move. And the CRC gate needs CRC-framed data; with uncoded symbols only G1 and
G3 remain, and G1 alone defeats the non-adaptive attacker but not the gate-aware one.

The remaining assumptions bound the generality of the results: a pilot-perturbing
attacker with an energy budget (a directional jammer or a data spoofer lies outside the
model); the block-fading channel families of the DeepSIC literature, with no link-level
simulation under a 3GPP model and LDPC coding; QPSK with 100 pilot symbols per user; and
DeepSIC as the only backbone tested, although the levels and gates are agnostic to the
per-user network.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
This paper presented a DeepSIC receiver that keeps its adaptable parameters at three
time-scales, consolidates from fast to slow memory only through blocks that pass three
label-free physics-consistency tests, pulls the fast level toward the slow projection,
and detects a rejected block with the slow memory. Against the pilot-poisoning attacks
that destroy online and meta-learned receivers, the attacked symbol error rate equals
the clean one at every budget, on four channel families, two system sizes and three
Doppler rates, with $86$--$99\%$ of poisoned blocks rejected and at most $1.7\%$ of clean
ones; without attack the receiver halves the error rate of online DeepSIC where
interference cancellation matters. The exposure of the slow memory to a bounded attack is
divided by its period and multiplied by the miss rate of the gate, and the bound holds
with measured constants. Two lessons follow: the defence lies in the memory, not in the
learner, and a gate tied to the attacker's own objective is what a gate-aware attacker
cannot evade without giving that objective up. The stated limits point to a re-detection
pass for accepted blocks, a jointly contractive fast learner, link-level simulation with
LDPC coding, and other backbones, to which the levels and gates apply unchanged.
